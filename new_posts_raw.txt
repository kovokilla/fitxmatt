--- Educational #1 ---
Protein is the most important nutrient for IT professionals. Here's why.

When you're doing high-intensity cognitive work all day, your brain runs on glucose — and your body will pull it from wherever it can, including muscle tissue if protein intake is too low.

Adequate protein preserves muscle while losing fat, keeps you fuller for longer (reducing the evening overeating cycle), and has the highest thermic effect of any macronutrient — meaning you burn more calories just digesting it.

For a desk worker trying to lose weight, hitting a daily protein target is the single highest-leverage nutritional habit you can build.

You don't need to track everything. Just track protein.

👉 See the real results: https://lnkd.in/eu5djaTU

--- Educational #2 ---
Why IT professionals gain weight even when they "don't eat that much."

Sedentary work dramatically lowers your total daily energy expenditure. A highly active person might burn 2,800 calories a day. A sedentary desk worker with the same body might burn 1,900.

The gap isn't the food. It's the movement. And most IT professionals don't account for how little energy their body is actually using during a desk-work day.

The solution isn't to eat like you're running marathons. It's to calibrate your nutrition to your actual activity level — which is different for every person depending on their schedule and lifestyle.

👉 See the real results: https://lnkd.in/eu5djaTU

--- Educational #3 ---
The 3pm energy crash is almost always a lunch problem.

High-carbohydrate lunches — sandwiches, pasta, rice bowls, most fast food — cause a blood sugar spike followed by a crash about two to three hours later.

For IT professionals, that crash lands right at 2-3pm, during the part of the afternoon that requires the most focused cognitive work.

The fix is simple: shift lunch toward higher protein and fat, lower refined carbs. The blood sugar curve flattens, the crash disappears, and you get three more productive hours back every single day.

One meal change. Significant daily impact.

👉 See the real results: https://lnkd.in/eu5djaTU

--- Educational #4 ---
Sleep is a nutrition issue. Here's what that means for IT professionals.

Poor sleep raises ghrelin (the hunger hormone) and lowers leptin (the fullness hormone). After a bad night — which is common during on-call rotations — you're physiologically hungrier and less able to feel full.

This means the day after an on-call callout, you will overeat. Not because of poor willpower, but because your hormones are pushing you toward it.

Knowing this lets you plan around it. You can't always fix the sleep, but you can build a nutrition structure that accounts for the hormonal fallout.

Information is leverage.

👉 See the real results: https://lnkd.in/eu5djaTU

--- Educational #5 ---
Walking after meals is one of the highest-ROI health habits for desk workers.

Ten minutes of walking after eating improves insulin sensitivity, blunts the post-meal blood sugar spike, and aids digestion. For IT professionals who can't always get to the gym, it's the most accessible form of movement available.

You don't need a gym. You don't need equipment. You need ten minutes and a pair of shoes you're already wearing.

If you do nothing else differently today, take a ten-minute walk after lunch. The cumulative effect over weeks is significant.
